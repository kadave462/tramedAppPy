How does this forecast work? Algorithm & example

The system reads the last 90 days of real sales from the database, fits a statistical model, and projects forward for the next 30 days. The forecast answers two questions: "How many units will we sell?" and "Do we need to reorder, and how much?"

The Algorithm — 3 Steps

1

Day-of-week multipliers (seasonal pattern)

Pharmacies are busier on certain days. The model computes a multiplier for each day of the week using all 90 days of history. If the overall daily average is 2 units but Monday's average is 3.2 units, the Monday multiplier = 3.2 ÷ 2.0 = 1.6. Sunday at 0.8 units → multiplier = 0.4.

multiplier[day] = avg_sales_on_that_day ÷ overall_daily_avg
2

Linear trend (last 30 days)

To find whether demand is rising, falling, or stable, the model removes the day-of-week noise (deseasonalises) and fits a straight line through the last 30 days using linear regression (numpy polyfit). The slope of that line is the trend.

deseasonalised_qty[day] = actual_qty ÷ multiplier[day_of_week]
trend_slope, intercept = polyfit(day_index, deseasonalised_qty)

Slope > +0.03 units/day → ↑ Increasing  |  Slope < −0.03 → ↓ Decreasing  |  Between → → Stable

3

Projection (next 30 days)

Each future day is predicted by applying the trend, then re-multiplying by that day's day-of-week multiplier. The result is clamped to zero (forecasts are never negative).

predicted[day] = max(0, trend_value × multiplier[day_of_week])

The 7d, 14d, and 30d totals in the table are the sums of these daily predictions.

R

Reorder recommendation

Based on current stock and the forecast, the system calculates how many days of stock remain and whether an order is needed before the supplier can deliver.

days_remaining = stock_qty ÷ avg_daily_sales
reorder_qty = (forecast_14d + avg_daily × 7 safety days) − stock_qty
Critical — ≤ 3 days left (less than lead time)
Urgent — 4–10 days left (within safety buffer)
Soon — 11–14 days left
OK — more than 14 days of stock

Step 1 — Seasonality

From 90 days of history: overall average = 2.2 units/day. Monday average = 3.5 units → multiplier = 3.5 ÷ 2.2 = 1.59. Sunday average = 0.9 units → multiplier = 0.9 ÷ 2.2 = 0.41.

Step 2 — Trend

After removing seasonality, linear regression on the last 30 days gives a slope of +0.04 units/day. Since 0.04 > 0.03, trend = ↑ Increasing.

Step 3 — Projection for next Monday

trend_value ≈ 2.2  ×  Monday multiplier (1.59) = 3.5 units predicted for Monday.

Summing all 14 days ahead gives a 14-day forecast of ~32 units.

Reorder decision

days_remaining = 20 ÷ 2.2 = 9.1 days

→ 9.1 days is between 3 and 10 → status = URGENT

reorder_qty = (32 + 2.2 × 7) − 20

           = (32 + 15.4) − 20

           = 27 units to order

The 14-day forecast covers the demand during the lead time + safety buffer (3 + 7 = 10 days), and the reorder quantity tops up stock to a comfortable level.

Limitations: The model assumes recent trends continue. It does not know about upcoming holidays, supplier stockouts, or new prescriptions. Treat the reorder quantity as a starting point — adjust based on your experience with each supplier.
