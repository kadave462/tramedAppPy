Forecasting Module and Logic

How the demand forecast works, the algorithm behind it, and a full worked example — June 2026

What This Module Does

The system reads the last 90 days of real sales from the database, fits a statistical model, and projects demand forward for the next 30 days. The forecast answers two questions:

  • 1. How many units will we sell? — per product, per day, over the next 7, 14, and 30 days.
  • 2. Do we need to reorder, and how much? — urgency level (Critical / Urgent / Soon / OK) and the recommended quantity to order from the supplier.

The module lives in forecasting.py and is called by the /forecast page and the Monday 08:00 automated WhatsApp summary.

The Algorithm — 3 Steps

1

Day-of-week multipliers (seasonal pattern)

Pharmacies are not equally busy every day. Mondays tend to be busier because patients collect medication at the start of the week; Sundays are quieter. The model captures this by computing a multiplier for each day of the week using all 90 days of sales history.

For example, if the overall daily average across all 90 days is 2.0 units, but Monday's average is 3.2 units, then:

multiplier[Monday] = 3.2 ÷ 2.0 = 1.6
multiplier[Sunday] = 0.8 ÷ 2.0 = 0.4

# General formula:
multiplier[day] = avg_sales_on_that_day ÷ overall_daily_avg

A multiplier above 1.0 means that day is busier than average. Below 1.0 means quieter. This is applied again in Step 3 when projecting future days.

2

Linear trend (last 30 days)

To find whether demand is rising, falling, or stable, the model first removes the day-of-week noise (called deseasonalisation) from the last 30 days of sales. It then fits a straight line through those corrected values using linear regression (numpy polyfit). The slope of that line is the trend.

# Remove day-of-week noise first:
deseasonalised_qty[day] = actual_qty ÷ multiplier[day_of_week]

# Then fit a line through the cleaned data:
trend_slope, intercept = polyfit(day_index, deseasonalised_qty)
↑
Slope > +0.03 units/day
Increasing
→
Between −0.03 and +0.03
Stable
↓
Slope < −0.03 units/day
Decreasing

The threshold of ±0.03 was chosen deliberately — it equates to roughly 1 extra unit per month, which is a meaningful change for a pharmacy product. Anything smaller is treated as noise.

3

Projection (next 30 days)

For each of the next 30 days, the model takes the trend line value for that day's index, then re-multiplies it by the day-of-week multiplier for that specific day (Monday gets ×1.6, Sunday gets ×0.4, etc.). The result is clamped to zero so the forecast never predicts negative sales.

predicted[day] = max(0, trend_value × multiplier[day_of_week])

The 7d, 14d, and 30d totals shown in the forecast table are simply the sums of these daily predictions over each window.

Reorder Recommendation Logic

Once the forecast is calculated, the system compares it against current stock to decide urgency and how much to order. Two constants are set in the code:

LEAD_TIME_DAYS = 3 # days between placing an order and receiving stock
SAFETY_DAYS = 7 # extra buffer days to always keep in stock
days_remaining = stock_qty ÷ avg_daily_sales

reorder_qty = (forecast_14d + avg_daily × SAFETY_DAYS) − stock_qty
Critical
≤ 3 days of stock remaining
Less than lead time — order will arrive after stockout. Order immediately.
Urgent
4–10 days of stock remaining
Within safety buffer window. Order within the next day or two.
Soon
11–14 days of stock remaining
Stock will last less than 2 weeks. Plan reorder this week.
OK
More than 14 days of stock
Stock is healthy. No action needed right now.

The reorder quantity formula targets having 14 days of forecasted demand plus a 7-day safety buffer in stock — then subtracts what is already on the shelf. This ensures enough stock arrives before the next order cycle.

Step 1 — Seasonality multipliers

From 90 days of history, the overall daily average is 2.2 units/day.

Monday average = 3.5 units → multiplier = 3.5 ÷ 2.2 = 1.59
Tuesday average = 2.8 units → multiplier = 2.8 ÷ 2.2 = 1.27
Wednesday avg = 2.4 units → multiplier = 2.4 ÷ 2.2 = 1.09
Thursday avg = 2.1 units → multiplier = 2.1 ÷ 2.2 = 0.95
Friday average = 1.8 units → multiplier = 1.8 ÷ 2.2 = 0.82
Saturday avg = 1.4 units → multiplier = 1.4 ÷ 2.2 = 0.64
Sunday average = 0.9 units → multiplier = 0.9 ÷ 2.2 = 0.41

Monday sells 59% more than the weekly average. Sunday sells 59% less. These multipliers are applied every time a future day is predicted.

Step 2 — Trend (linear regression)

After dividing each of the last 30 days' sales by its day-of-week multiplier to remove weekly noise, a straight line is fitted through the cleaned data.

trend_slope = +0.04 units/day

Since 0.04 > 0.03 → trend label = ↑ Increasing

+0.04 units/day means demand is growing by roughly 1.2 extra units per month. Not explosive growth, but a consistent upward signal.

Step 3 — Projection (next Monday as example)

To predict next Monday's sales, the model reads the trend value at that future day index, then multiplies by the Monday multiplier.

trend_value for next Monday ≈ 2.2 units (from the fitted line)
× Monday multiplier (1.59)
= 3.5 units predicted for that Monday

Repeating for all 14 days → 14-day total forecast = ~32 units

Each day of the 30-day window gets its own prediction this way. The 7d, 14d, and 30d figures in the table are the running totals.

Reorder Decision

days_remaining = 20 ÷ 2.2 = 9.1 days

→ 9.1 is between 3 and 10 → status = URGENT

reorder_qty = (forecast_14d + avg_daily × SAFETY_DAYS) − stock

           = (32 + 2.2 × 7) − 20

           = (32 + 15.4) − 20

           = 27 units to order

The 14-day forecast (32 units) covers demand during the lead time + safety buffer (3 + 7 = 10 days). Ordering 27 units tops stock up to a comfortable level without over-ordering.

Where This Runs in the System

Forecast page — /forecast

Runs all_product_forecasts() for every product on every page load. Shows the full table with urgency, days remaining, trend arrows, and reorder quantities. Includes WhatsApp buttons to send reorder messages to suppliers.

Automated WhatsApp summary — every Monday at 08:00

APScheduler triggers weekly_reorder_summary() which calls the forecast, builds a summary message, and sends it via Twilio WhatsApp to the pharmacist's number. Covers all Critical / Urgent / Soon products in one message.

Chart modal — individual product view

Clicking "View Chart" on any row calls /api/product-forecast/<id>, which runs forecast_product() for that single product and returns the daily arrays for Chart.js to render the 30-day actual + 14-day forecast line chart.

Limitations and Known Gaps

  • ✗ No holiday awareness. The model treats public holidays (Genocide Memorial Day, Independence Day, Christmas) like any other day. A Monday that is also a holiday will still get the Monday multiplier, which will overestimate sales.
  • ✗ No supplier stockout memory. If a product ran out of stock last month, those days show zero sales — not because demand was zero, but because there was nothing to sell. This suppresses the average and underestimates reorder quantities for products that stockout often.
  • ✗ New products have no history. Products added recently (fewer than 30 days of data) get unreliable trend estimates. The forecast still runs but should be treated with caution.
  • ✗ Trend extrapolation only. The model cannot predict a sudden spike (e.g. a flu season, a community event nearby). Treat the forecast as a baseline, not a guarantee.
  • → Recommendation: Use the reorder quantities as a starting point. Adjust up or down based on your knowledge of upcoming events, supplier reliability, and product expiry dates.
